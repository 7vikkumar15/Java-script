A submarine pen (or U-boat bunker) is a heavily reinforced, concrete bunker designed during World War II to protect submarines from aerial bombing, serving as a secure, land-based or water-based facility for docking, repair, and maintenance. Primarily built by Germany in occupied Atlantic ports like Brest, Lorient, and Saint-Nazaire, these "monstrous" structures featured roofs up to 11 feet thick and walls designed to withstand massive, deep-penetration bombs. Construction involved massive amounts of concrete and often relied on forced labour or concentration camp inmates. Today, many are abandoned, but some still exist as relics, museums, or, in the case of the Bordeaux base, as a unique digital art center. Key Features of Submarine Pens: Purpose: To provide a safe harbor for submarines, which were highly vulnerable to air attack while docked. Construction: Massive, thick-walled concrete structures. Facilities: Often included workshops, offices, medical facilities, storage for explosives/fuel, and generators. Types: Included dry-dock pens where boats were lifted out of the water, and wet-dock pens where boats floated inside. Locations: Major sites included Brest, Lorient, Saint-Nazaire, and La Pallice in France, as well as Bergen and Trondheim in Norway.
